(sketch: reaction per g catalyst vs dispersion — a maximum at intermediate dispersion: the max rate is where 4N adsorbate clusters are spread out, minimizing interactions.)

(iv) H₂ chemisorption is not applicable on Fe since H₂ exhibits activated adsorption on Fe, and thus it will not give accurate results [Vannice].

Dispersion on an Fe catalyst can be measured via CO chemisorption. CO adsorbs non-dissociatively but can adsorb linearly on a single atom site or at a bridge where it binds to 2 sites ↳ requires 2 isotherms for uptake (for reversible uptake).

CO chemisorption:

  1. CO is introduced and the CO uptake (µmol/g_cat) is measured as a function of pressure.
  2. If a support is present, then blank runs with the support are also measured.
  3. Reversible & irreversible uptake are deconvoluted:
    • reversible → weak adsorption from the support (Henry’s law regime @ high pressures) ↳ low coverage proportional to adsorbate pressure
    • irreversible → strong chemisorption on the reduced metal surface (Langmuirian isotherm → high-pressure regime)
  4. Dispersion is calculated using the total no. of atoms (metal loading) & the surface-site information from the CO uptake.
  5. The average crystallite size is then calculated as, for the spherical-particle assumption [Vannice]:
D=(d0.6​)(Am​Vm​​)D=dispersion,d=crystallite size,Vm​=bulk volume density,Am​=bulk area density

For TEM: given a histogram of # of particles with a given size → the average can be calculated via a binomial-distribution fit on the data set.

Surface-averaged & number-averaged crystallite sizes would be similar for materials where surface exposure is not reflective of their activity for chemisorption, i.e. non-metallic sites or single-atom sites.

(v) Ideal HCP axis-size ratio (sketch: the HCP cell with an equilateral-triangle base of side a and an atom at height c/2 above its centroid):

h=a2−(2a​)2​=a43​​;x=32​h=3​1​a;2c​=a2−x2​=a2−31​a2​=a32​​⇒ac​=38​​​

Interstitial sites exist between the spaces within a unit cell:

  • The close-packed cubic cell (CC) has 4 octahedral sites (coordinated to 6 atoms) & 8 tetrahedral sites (coordinated to 4 atoms).
  • HCP has 6 octahedral sites & 12 tetrahedral sites.

(sketch: a tetrahedral site of radius r nestled between spheres of radius R, inside a cube of edge a with face diagonal a2​ and body diagonal a3​.)

2R=a2​⇒R=2a2​​;(R+r)=2a3​​∴2a2​​+r=2a3​​⇒r=2a(3​−2​)​=0.159a or r=0.225R​

For an atom to occupy a tetrahedral void, its radius must be 0.225 times the radius of Fe.

HCP and FCC crystal structures are quite similar [where the FCC(111) plane corresponds to the close-packed plane of HCP (001)] } therefore the voids are similar.

Question 5

Method development to measure the SA of a Pt catalyst during the WGS reaction via steady-state isotopic transient kinetic analysis (SSITKA).

WGS reaction: CO+H2​O⇌CO2​+H2​

Theory: detecting the time scale of evolution of labelled products & correlating it with the evolution of labelled surface active species (via time-resolved FTIR).

Gas-delivery system (sketch: Ne/¹³CO and Ar/CO lines into a 4-way switching valve → reactor cell (T-controlled) → MS analyzer).

  • → We know CO adsorbs on Pt (usually linearly) → therefore we can calculate the total moles of CO adsorbed on Pt by ¹²CO/¹³CO isotope switching to perform CO oxidation, to get the total surface area of the catalyst.
  • → However, not all surface sites are “active” for the WGS reaction, and therefore we can get an idea about the active metal sites via isotopic transient measurements as such:
    1. The isotope tracer (Ne) & ¹³CO-labelled gas are introduced into the reactor cell via the 4-way switching valve.
    2. The mean surface residence time of ¹³CO is calculated by integrating the area between the Ne trace (which elutes first) and ¹³CO₂ (the WGS product).
    3. The surface conc. of carbon-containing active intermediates is then calculated as the product of the rate & the mean surface residence time (which can be corrected for any support influences).
    4. This value can then be compared with the # of total surface atoms (via H₂ chemisorption). Assuming a certain no. of surface sites required to bind with the intermediate (i.e. one for CO binding with Pt via top sites), an estimate of the “active” surface area for WGS on the catalyst can be determined.
