① (Ideal) gas treatments

Divide them into translational, rotational, vibrational and electronic (DFT) parts:

qvib​=i∏modes​1−e−hνi​βe−hνi​β/2​(per molecule; includes ZPE);qrot​=σβℏ2I​(linear, per molecule) qtrans​=(h22πmkB​T​)3/2V(per molecule);qel​=e−EDFT​/kB​T(total energy) Qtot​=N!qtransN​​qvibN​qrotN​e−EDFT​/kB​T;Ftot​=−kB​TlnQtot​,μ=∂N∂Ftot​​=−kB​T∂N∂​(lnQtot​)

With lnN!≈NlnN−N:

μ=−kB​Tln​(h22πmkB​T​)3/2kB​T/P(NV​)​​​−kB​Tlnqvib​−kB​Tlnqrot​+∂N∂EDFT​​​

Standard state: μ∘=μ(P=P∘):

μ∘=−23​kB​Tln(h22πmkB​T​)−kB​Tln(P∘kB​T​)−kB​Tlnqvib​−kB​Tlnqrot​+∂N∂EDFT​​∴μ=μ∘+kB​Tln(P∘P​)

(use this to calculate K‘s in the microkinetic model). Equivalently, μ∘=εDFT​+ZPE−TSˉ, with Sˉ=∂S/∂N, and ∂N∂EDFT​​=∂N∂​(NεDFT​)=εDFT​ (per molecule).

③ Surface processes

(a) First-order treatment: calculate harmonic modes on the surface (3Natoms​ modes) and use the harmonic partition function:

Qtot​=qvibN​e−EDFT​/kB​TN!(Ns​−N)!Ns​!​ μ=∂N∂Etot​​=μ∘ (includes ZPE)−kB​Tln[i∏modes​1−e−hνi​βe−hνi​β/2​]+∂N∂EDFT​​​​+activitykB​Tln(θ∗​θ​)​​;θ∗​=1−θA​−θB​−θC​ ∴μ=μ∘+kB​Tln(θ∗​θ​)​(for a TS, do not include the reaction coordinate)

Caution

This approach fails for low-frequency modes (νi​≲150 cm−1): low frequencies imply low diffusion barriers — anharmonic. (Sketch: a corrugated potential V(x) along the surface; the frequency is curvature​; at higher T, adsorbates access anharmonic states or are not even bound.) These modes make the largest contribution to the entropy.

Crude correction: take all modes with νi​≲150 cm−1 and assume their entropies are all equal to that of a mode with ν=150 cm−1 (!?).

(b) Better treatments of low-frequency modes

(i) Particle-in-a-box (for adsorbates). Sketch: a box from 0 to L; in 2D, free diffusion in a box, but the molecules are still localized.

In 2D:En,l​=2mπ2ℏ2​[Lx2​n2​+Ly2​l2​],n,l=1,2,… qtrans(per molecule)​=n=1∑∞​l=1∑∞​exp[−2mkB​Tπ2ℏ2​(Lx2​n2​+Ly2​l2​)]≈∫0∞​∫0∞​e−2mkB​TL2π2ℏ2n2​e−2mkB​TL2π2ℏ2l2​dndl(if Lx​=Ly​=L)=h22πmkB​T​Abox​

where Abox​=L2 = the area of free diffusion for each adsorbate (define this for the area that you care about around the active site).

Qtrans​=qtrans,boxN​;μtrans​=−kB​Tln[h22πmkB​T​Abox​]

Important

μ∘=−kB​Tln[h22πmkB​T​Abox​] Replace the low-frequency translational modes with this; everything else is the same. Useful for zeolites and surfaces at high coverages.

(ii) Two-dimensional ideal gases (simple adsorption/desorption, weakly bound adsorbates):

qtrans​=Ah22πmkB​T​(per molecule);Qtot​=N!qtransN​​qvibN​qrotN​e−EDFT​/kB​T

(identify the low-frequency modes and the high-frequency modes). This gives NA​=θas​​, with θ=N/Ns​:

μ=−kB​Tln[h22πmkB​T​as​]−kB​Tln(qvib​qrot​)+∂N∂EDFT​​+kB​Tlnθ(qvib​ includes ZPE) μ=μ∘+kB​Tlnθ​

There is no θ∗​ because ideal gases have no volume (so no site-blocking term).

  • → Not correct for real molecules at high coverages.
  • → Still approximately true if θ∗​≈1: μ=μ∘+kB​Tln(θ/θ∗​).

Rough correction: change A→A−Naads​ (the area excluded by adsorbates). Then

NA​→NA−Naads​​=NNs​as​−Naads​​=as​θ(1−as​aads​​θ)​

⇒μ∘ is the same: μ=μ∘+kB​Tlnθ∗​θ​, with θ∗​=1−as​aads​​θ.

If the coverage is getting too high (θ∗​≪1), consider the particle-in-a-box (or a different model).
