Recall

  1. A2​(g)+∗⇌A2∗​ (QE)
  2. A2∗​+∗⇌2A∗ (RLS)
  3. A∗+B(g)⇌AB(g)+∗ (QE)

Sketch: lnr vs ΔE1​ volcanoes for ΔEA2​​=−2.5, −1.5 and 0 eV, plus the energy diagram (step 2 is the RLS): A2​(g) → A2∗​ (well depth ΔEA2∗​​) → TS (intrinsic barrier) → 2A∗ (ΔE1​).

  • Moving A2∗​ will increase the energy of the TS → so ΔE1​ has to move proportionally down to keep the same barrier.
  • A2∗​ corresponds to the binding energy → if A2∗​ goes really high up, then the RLS will change.

Volcano

Assuming the first step is a lumped step → we don’t need the binding/activation energy of step 2!

Sketch: lnr vs ΔE1​ — the true volcano for γ=10−2 (and a lower one for γ=0.99), with leg ② on the left (step 2 is RLS) and leg ① on the right (in this region step 1 is RLS); the Sabatier bound is the dashed curve above.

The different “legs” of the volcano (① and ②) correspond (approximately) to different assumptions about the RLS.

Leg ①: assume step 1 is the RLS (as before; γ = overall approach to equilibrium):

r=r1​=k1​PA2​​θ∗2​−k−1​θA2​;θA​=K2−1​PB​PAB​​θ∗​;r=k1​PA2​​θ∗2​(1−γ) r(1)​=(1+K2−1​PB​PAB​​)2k1​PA2​​​(1−γ)(sketched on the graph above)

Sabatier: assume maximum rates ↳ θ∗​=1: rSab​=k1​PA2​​(1−γ) (upper bound).

Leg ②: assume step 2 is the RLS:

r=21​r2​=21​(k2​PB​θA​−k−2​PAB​θ∗​);step 1 is QE: θA​=K1​PA2​′​​θ∗​,θA​+θ∗​=1 r(2)​=21​k2​PB​θA​(1−γ​)=1+K1​PA2​′​​21​k2​K1​PA2​′​​​(1−γ​)<upper bound 21​k2​PB​…

Notes

  1. The legs (sides) are determined by assuming different RLS’s. The maximum of the volcano is a transition between different RLS’s. [Each RLS has its own volcano — a competition between rate constants and coverage effects.]
  2. The limiting expressions approximate the true volcano except where the volcanoes cross.
  3. Sabatier rates are useful if γ≪1.
  4. Volcanoes assuming only 1 RLS can wildly overestimate rates. ↳ Volcanoes with > 2 steps may have many legs!
