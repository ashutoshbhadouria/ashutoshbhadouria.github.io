Simple kinetic-energy functional

For non-interacting fermions in a box:

NE​=ekin​(ρ)=6mπ2ℏ2​ρ2(energy per particle)
  • ρ(x)dx is the number of particles in length dx
  • ekin​(ρ)(ρdx) is the KE in length dx
  • Total energy is EKE​[ρ(x)]=∫ekin​(ρ)ρdx over all space — the general KE expression

If there is potential energy or interactions: E[ρ(x)]=∫etot​(ρ)ρdx

Example 1: reproduce the PIB energies for N particles

E[ρ]=∫0L​6mπ2ℏ2​ρ3dx;constraint: ∫0L​ρ(x)dx=N(to avoid the trivial solution)

Use Lagrange multipliers. Define

F[ρ]≡E[ρ]−μ(∫0L​ρ(x)dx−N),μ=Lagrange multiplier

Note

∂ρ∂​∫F(ρ,∇ρ)dr=∂ρ∂f​−∂∇ρ∂f​, where F is a function of ρ and ∇ρ.

Then minimize:

∂ρ∂F(ρ)​=0=2mπ2ℏ2​ρmin2​−μ(1∂ρ∂​∫ρdx​​−0∂ρ∂N​​​)∴μ=2mπ2ℏ2​ρmin2​ ∫0L​π2ℏ22mμ​​dx=N⇒π2ℏ22mμ​​=LN​⇒ρmin​=LN​​ ∴Emin​=6mπ2ℏ2​L3N3​L​

This approximation improves for large N (energies in units of π2ℏ2/2mL2):

Principle

DFT gives more accurate results for systems that are similar to the systems for which the functional was derived. However, results are surprisingly good for different system types as well.

Example 2: delta-function spike

Sketch: V(x) in a box from 0 to L with a sharp spike at L/2.

Properties of δ:

  1. ∫0∞​δ(x)dx=1 (thin and tall)
  2. ∫0L​f(x)δ(x−2L​)dx=f(2L​)∫0L​δ(x−2L​)dx=f(2L​)

New functional:

E[ρ]=∫0L​6mπ2ℏ2​ρ3dx+external PEα∫δ(x−2L​)ρdx​​

Lagrange functional:

F[ρ]≡E[ρ]−μ(∫0L​ρdx−N) ∂ρ∂F​=−μ+2mπ2ℏ2​ρmin2​+→0∂ρ∂​(ρ(2L​)α)​​∴ρmin​=π2ℏ22mμ​​=LN​(constant)

δ does not affect the density.

Emin​=6mπ2ℏ2​(LN​)3L+LαN​1∫0L​δ(x−2L​)dx​​∴Emin​=6mπ2ℏ2​(LN​)3L+LαN​

δ does add α/L per particle (α = constant) → physically, an obstacle (or nucleus) in the center will act as a δ function: it will affect the energy every time an e− crosses it in the particle-in-a-box example.

Example 3: linear potential

Sketch: V(x) rising linearly from 0 at x=0 to Vmax​ at x=L:

Vext​(x)=LVmax​x​ etot​[ρ]=6mπ2ℏ2​ρ2+Lx​Vmax​E[ρ]=6mπ2ℏ2​∫0L​ρ3dx+∫0L​LVmax​​xρdx

Lagrange multiplier:

∂ρ∂F​=0=2mπ2ℏ2​ρmin2​+LVmax​x​−μ∴ρmin​=π2ℏ22m​(μ−LVmax​x​)​(ρmin​ is not constant)

Finding μ from ∫0L​ρmin​dx=N:

∫0L​π2ℏ22m​(μ−LVmax​x​)​dx=Nu-sub,u=μ−Vmax​x/L​(μ−Vmax​)3/2−μ3/2=−23​LVmax​N​2mπ2ℏ2​​

Either solve numerically or solve for small Vmax​/μ:

μ≈2mπ2ℏ2​L2N2​+O(Vmax​)↪ρmin​(x)=LN​[1−LVmax​x​π2ℏ2N22mL2​]​

Sketch: ρmin​ decreasing linearly from x=0 to x=L — the electric field pushes on the particle, so there is less probability for it to be on that side of the box.

Write γ=LN​ and β=LVmax​​π2ℏ2N22mL2​, so ρ=γ(1−βx):

E[ρmin​]​=6mπ2ℏ2​∫0L​γ3(1−βx)3dx+LγVmax​​∫0L​x(1−βx)dx=−β1​6mπ2ℏ2​4γ3​≈−4βL (small β)[(1−βL)4−1]​​+LVmax​​γ(2L2​−β3L3​)​ Emin​=6mL2π2ℏ2​N3+21​Vmax​N​for small Vmax​

↪ So we add 21​Vmax​ to the energy of each particle (on average).

HW preview: the 1D Thomas–Fermi atom

Sketch: a wall at x=0 with a Coulomb potential Vext​(x)=−α/x; ρmin​ decays away from the wall.

μ<0→ need absolute values (replace μ=−∣μ∣).

Answer:

E[ρ]=6mπ2ℏ2​∫0∞​ρ3dx−α∫0∞​xρ(x)​dx 0=∂ρ∂F​→ρmin​(x)=(π2ℏ22m​)1/2(μ+xα​)1/2 ⇒N=∫0∞​ρmin​dx=(π2ℏ22m​)1/2∣μ∣1/2∫0∞​(xα/∣μ∣​−1)1/2dx

ρ can never be imaginary! So truncate the domain to α/∣μ∣ to keep ρmin​ real:

⇓μ=−2ℏ2m​N2α2​ Emin​[ρmin​]=6mL2π2ℏ2​N3+6mπ2ℏ2​diverges∫0α/∣μ∣​(π2ℏ22m​)3/2∣μ∣3/2(xα/∣μ∣​−1)3/2dx​​

The integral diverges because we are modelling an atom in 1D → larger N means the particles start to pack into the lower-potential region with nowhere for them to go → unphysically packed.

Interactions between particles

1) Coulomb: classic electrostatic interactions. E∼4πε0​xe2​, repulsion between electrons.

Electrostatic functional (x′ is a dummy variable; the 21​ avoids double counting):

ECoul​=21​4πε0​e2​∬∣x−x′∣ρ(x)ρ(x′)​dxdx′

2) Exchange: a purely QM property/interaction (attractive, and between charged particles).

↪ Fully accounted for in the TISE, but DFT requires extra terms. Simple approximation with dimensional analysis:

Eexch​∼∫ε0​e2​ρndx,n=2 gives dimensional consistency

Putting it together (in 1D)

E[ρ]=6mπ2ℏ2​∫ρ3dx+21​4πε0​e2​∬∣x−x′∣ρ(x)ρ(x′)​dxdx′−Cx​4πε0​e2​∫ρ2dx+∫Vext​ρdx

where Vext​ could be −xα​, 21​kx2, …
