TE is O(Δx2) ↳ second order in mesh spacing. Thus the difference operator is second-order accurate.

  • → If f is single-valued, finite and cont., the Taylor series for f is convergent ↳ this means that as Δx→∞, the TE for a higher-order approx. will be smaller than that of a lower-order TE.
  • → Similarly, from the Taylor expansion we get the “central difference”:
Δx2f(x+Δx,y)−2f(x,y)+f(x−Δx,y)​≈∂x2∂2f​;TE=O(Δx2)=4!2Δx2​∂x4∂4f​+6!2Δx4​⋯

Explicit method

Finding the value for n+1, when the solution @ n is assumed to be known (this is the given initial cond. T(x,t=0)=T0​) ↓ this just becomes an algebraic expression. I.e. FDEs which use no information at the new time level give uncoupled ODEs:

ΔtTin+1​−Tin​​=α(Δx2Ti+1n​−2Tin​+Ti−1n​​)

Implicit method

→ Coupled m equations where spatial information about the new time level is needed (but we only know the solution at time level n) ↓ therefore all eqs need to be solved simultaneously → Ax=b.

We could also use a generalized approach of both explicit & implicit → this gives second-order accurate in both space & time.

Now based on the PDE → there are general requirements for BCs in order to get a solution:

  • ↳ Hyperbolic, 1st order → need BC only at points where characteristic base curves enter the domain.
  • ↳ Parabolic/elliptical → BC at every point on the boundary of the spatial domain.

Consistency, stability & convergence

  • Discretization error → replacing PDEs w/ FDEs.
  • Stability error → round-off errors (finite digits used in computations).
  • Truncation error → O(Δt,Δx2), error due to approximation.

1) Consistency

Δx→0Δt→0​lim​(FDE−PDE)in​=Δx→0lim​(TE)in​=0

How well does the FDE locally approx. the PDE. (sketch: a wavy curve matching a parabola only near its minimum — “consistent here (locally)”.)

How to check?

∂t∂T​=α(∂x2∂2T​);ΔtTin+1​−Tin​​=α(Δx2Ti+1n​−2Tin​+Ti−1n​​)

Expand all terms via Taylor series & then substitute:

Ti+1n​=Tin​+(∂x∂T​)ni​Δx+⋯;Ti−1n​=Tin​−(∂x∂T​)ni​Δx+⋯;Tin+1​=Tin​+(∂t∂T​)in​Δt+⋯

↓ Let Δt→0, Δx→0 → if the same as the PDE, then consistent.

  • ↳ Unconditional vs. conditional consistency: unconditional ↓ no dependence on the rates of Δx & Δt; conditional ↳ depends on the relative rates of Δx & Δt→0.
  • ↳ Since Δx & Δt are finite computationally → consistency alone does not guarantee accuracy; thus a consistent soln. still behaves diff. than the PDE.

2) (Numerical) stability

  • When the time domain is involved (i.e. accumulation or growth of errors) (parabolic & hyperbolic PDEs).
  • Depends solely on the FDE; can be bounded (i.e. don’t grow). (sketch: an oscillation decaying under a bounding envelope — “bounded”.)
  • The computational domain must include all of the analytical domain.
dimensionless parameterϕ​​=Δx2αΔt​≤21​→conditionally stable

3) Convergence

If the total error (discretization + stability) → 0 as Δx→0, Δt→0.

  • If the FDE is convergent, then a numerical soln. of any accuracy can be obtained by changing the grid spacing & time step.
  • The FDE must give the same solution as the PDE in some limiting cases.
