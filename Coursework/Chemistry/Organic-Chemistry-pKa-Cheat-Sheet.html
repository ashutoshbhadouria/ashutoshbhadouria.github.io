Page 2 — inductive effects, reactivity and reactions

Inductive effects affect pKa: F₃C–COOH ~1; F₂CH–COOH [?] 1.34; FCH₂–COOH 2.59; CH₃–COOH 4.76.

Reactivity (increasing upward): amide (RCONH₂) < ester (RCOOR′) < acid anhydride (RCOOCOR′) < acyl chloride (RCOCl).

Reactions

  1. Grignard + carbonyl → alcohol (primary, sec., tertiary). (sketch: vinyl-MgBr adding to cyclobutanone → alkoxide → tertiary allylic alcohol.)
  2. Organo + CO₂ → carbonyl/carboxylic acid. (sketch: PhLi + CO₂ → PhCO₂⁻ → (H⁺) → PhCOOH.) (sketch: the HOMO (sp) of the carbanion attacking the LUMO π* of CO₂.)
  3. Oxidising agents: primary alcohol →[O] aldehyde →[O] carboxylic acid; sec. alcohol →[O] ketone.
  4. Carbonyls:
    • Forming amides: (sketch: isobutyryl chloride + NH₃ → tetrahedral intermediate → isobutyramide + NH₄Cl.)
    • Forming esters: (sketch: benzaldehyde-derived intermediate with MeOH → methyl benzoate [?].)
    • Acid catalyzed: (sketch: protonation of C=O activates it for nucleophile attack → tetrahedral product.) Base catalyzed: (sketch: ⁻OH attacking an amide → tetrahedral intermediate → amide reformed / hydrolysis.)
    • Alcohol + carbonyl → acetal: (sketch: 2,2-dimethyl-1,3-propanediol + cyclopentanone → spiro 1,3-dioxane.)
    • Forming carboxylic acid — base-catalyzed hydrolysis: (sketch: methyl benzoate + NaOH → benzoate → (HCl) → benzoic acid, via the tetrahedral intermediate.) From amides — acid catalyzed: (sketch: N-phenyl amide (resonance stabilized) protonated, water attacks, proton transfers, aniline leaves → carboxylic acid.)
    • Forming imines: (sketch: aldehyde + MeNH₂ → carbinolamine → loss of water → iminium → imine.) Iminium ion: (sketch: pyrrolidine-derived N⁺=CH₂.)
  5. Wittig rxn: C=O → C=CH₂. (sketch: Ph₃P⁺CH₃ Br⁻ + NaH → ylide Ph₃P⁺–CH₂⁻ → with cyclohexanone → methylenecyclohexane.)

Pages 3–4 — amino acids

Proteins are large polymers made from amino acids. (sketch: general structure for proteins, N–CH(R₁)–C(=O)–[NH–CH(R_N)–C(=O)]ₙ–OH; amino acid general structure H₂N–CH(R)–COOH; alanine drawn as a Fischer projection.) These are in the L configuration. Peptides & proteins are made from amino acids via condensation. (sketch: two amino acids joining by an amide bond + H₂O.)

Hydrophobic: (1) glycine; (2) alanine; (3) proline; (4) valine; (5) leucine; (6) isoleucine; (7) methionine.

Aromatic R groups: (8) phenylalanine; (9) tyrosine; (10) tryptophan (“in turkey”).

Polar, uncharged: (11) serine; (12) threonine; (13) cysteine; (14) asparagine (side-chain amide — neutral molecule); (15) glutamine.

Negatively charged R groups: (16) aspartate; (17) glutamate.

(Positively charged, unlabelled on the sheet:) (18) lysine; (19) arginine; (20) histidine.

(All drawn as zwitterions, H₃N⁺–CH(R)–COO⁻.)
