Midterm 1 cheat sheet for CBE 176, electrochemical engineering (UC Berkeley). Three handwritten pages; the third repeats the title and continues with transport.

Page 1 — “CBE 176: MT#1 Cheat Sheet”

  • Red Cat → reduction @ cathode (e⁻ →); An Ox → oxidation @ anode (→ e⁻)
  • F=96485 C/mol
  • e⁻ flow from low potential → high potential (high free energy → low free energy)

Electroneutrality

  • iAAnode​+iACathode​=0 (i = current density [mA/cm²]; A = surface area)
  • ∑i​zi​ci​=0 (zi​ = charge on species; ci​ = conc. of species)
  • i=F∑zi​Ni​=F(z+​N+​+z−​N−​) — flux: diffusion, migration, convection
  • Mi​=nFsi​(MWi​)Q​ (total mass consumed/formed); Q=∫I(t)dt=I⋅t [=] mAh [sign convention written as reduction rxn]
  • Total e⁻ =FQ​⋅(6.023×1023); W=∫I(t)V(t)dt
  • Faradaic efficiency =Qtotal​Qdesired​​×100

Thermodynamics

Equilibrium / open-circuit potential: ΔGrxn∘​=−nFUrxn∘​ [U∘>0, ΔG<0 → spontaneous]

μˉ​i​=μi​+zi​FΦ;μi​=f(N,T,P);μˉ​e−​=−FΦ=−FU∘;μe−​=0;μˉ​i​=μi​(for an uncharged species)

η=Φ−U; Φ>U → decreases energy of e⁻ (when a positive potential is applied) → e⁻ transfer from reactants to electrode → anodic process/oxidation.

Vcell​=Uright​−Uleft​ @ equilibrium [Vcell​>0 for a galvanic cell].

Phase equilibrium (sketch: a cell β | S | ε with phases α and α′):

  • μˉ​iα​=μˉ​iβ​ [adjacent phases have equal (electro)chemical potentials of the same species ‘i’] } same species, diff. phase rule
  • U=Φα′−Φα; FU=μe−α​−μe−α′​ [left − right]
  • μ are Gf​ values from the NBS table @ 1 bar, 25 °C, 1 mol/kg
  • For a strong electrolyte → we assume complete dissociation & therefore can use the starting species.

Pourbaix diagram (sketch: V vs. SHE against pH — water stable between the O₂ line (1.23 V at pH 0) and the H₂ line (0 V), both sloping down; 0.816 and −0.828 V marked at pH 7 and 14; a vertical line has no V dependence (no e⁻), a horizontal line no pH dependence (no H⁺ or OH⁻); 0.401 V marked.)

  • M↔Mn++ne− [horizontal line]
  • M+nH2​O↔M(OH)n​+nH++ne−; M+nH2​O↔MOn​+2nH++ne− } slanted
  • M+nH2​O↔MOnn−​+nH++ne− [vertical]

Transcriber’s note: the “vertical” example as written still transfers electrons; a vertical Pourbaix line is a reaction with H⁺ but no e⁻.

Page 2 — kinetics and activity corrections

Kinetics: i=i0​[exp(RTαA​Fηs​​)−exp(RT−αc​Fηs​​)]; αA​=1−αc​. i=−κ∇Φ; we want high κ, high current, low η.

Deviations from 1 mol/kg molalities:

  • Non-ionizing solutes/weak electrolytes: μi​=μi∘​+RTln(m0​mi​​)+RTln(γi​) (mi​ = molality of i)
  • Ionizing solutes/strong electrolytes: μi​=μi∘​+νRTln(mA​γ±​)+RTln(ν+ν+​​ν−ν−​​); ν=ν+​+ν−​
  • Solvents: μ0​=μ0∘​−ν(MW)RTmA​ϕ (φ = osmotic coefficient)
  • Gases: μA​=μA∘​+RTln(pA​); pA​=xA​PfA​
  • Solids: (pure) μA​=μA∘​; (alloys) μA​=μA∘​+RTlnaA​ (aA​ = activity of A in alloy)

Deviations from T & P:

  • Pressure: U−U∘=−nF2.303RT​log(P∘P​); ln(x)=2.303log(x); @ n=1, 25 °C ⇒ U−U∘=60 mVlog(P∘P​)
  • Temp.: ΔH=ΔG−TΔS=−T2∂T∂(ΔG/T)​ [Gibbs–Helmholtz]; closed system: ΔU=nFS∘​(T−T∘) → small temp. changes

Transcriber’s note: ΔH=ΔG−TΔS is as written; the identity is ΔG=ΔH−TΔS, i.e. ΔH=ΔG+TΔS.
