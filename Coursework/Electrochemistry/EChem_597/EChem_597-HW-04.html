Question 1

Hemicylinder electrode [W 5.6] (sketch: a hemicylinder WE of radius r0​ with the CE at rCE​, a finite distance away from the WE).

(a) Assuming uniform composition, ∇2ϕ=0. In cylindrical geometry:

∇2ϕ=r1​∂r∂​(r∂r∂ϕ​)+r21​∂θ2∂2ϕ​+∂z2∂2ϕ​=0

↳ Considering only the radial direction we get r1​∂r∂​(r∂r∂ϕ​)=0, with BCs: ϕ=V @ r=r0​; ϕ=0 @ r=rc​.

(b) Solve for the ohmic resistance (RΩ​). Obtaining the ϕ profile:

∂r∂​(r∂r∂ϕ​)=0⇒r∂r∂ϕ​=c1​⇒∂r∂ϕ​=rc1​​⇒ϕ=c1​lnr+c2​

@ r=r0​, ϕ=V ⇒ V=c1​lnr0​+c2​; @ r=rCE​, ϕ=0 ⇒ 0=c1​lnrCE​+c2​. Subtracting:

V=c1​lnrCE​r0​​⇒c1​=ln(rCE​r0​​)V​;c2​=ln(rCE​r0​​)−VlnrCE​​ ∴ϕ=ln(rCE​r0​​)V​lnr−ln(rCE​r0​​)V​lnrCE​=ln(rCE​r0​​)V​ln(rCE​r​)

RΩ​=V/iΩ​ (assuming no charge-transfer resistance), with

iΩ​=−κ∂r∂ϕ​​r=r0​​;∂r∂ϕ​​r=r0​​=r0​c1​​=ln(rCE​r0​​)V​⋅r0​1​ ∴RΩ​=−κVr0​ln(r0​/rCE​)1​V​=κr0​​ln(r0​rCE​​)=RΩ​​(in Ωcm2[?])

WE surface area: πr0​l (l = length of cylinder):

RΩ′​=πr0​lRΩ​​=κπl1​ln(r0​rCE​​)=RΩ′​​(in Ω[?])

Transcriber’s note: the margin units read “in Ω” for RΩ​ and “in Ω cm²” for RΩ′​; they look swapped, so they are shown here the other way round with [?].

As rCE​→∞: RΩ​=κr0​​ln(r0​rCE​​)⇒limrCE​→∞​RΩ​=∞ ← if the CE is at an infinite distance away, there is infinite ohmic resistance.

(c)

  • For 1D: RΩ​=κL​ → this assumes that the CE is at distance L.
  • For hemispherical: RΩ​=κr0​​ → this assumes the CE is at ∞.
  • RΩ​ for hemicylindrical assumes rCE​ at a finite distance away ↓ comparing hemisphere to hemicylinder: ln(r0​rCE​​)→0 as r0​→∞.

So cylindrical approaches spherical behaviour for large r0​.

Linear & anodic Tafel kinetics.

(a) Rct​=∂i∂ηs​​​i=iavg​​. Cathodic Tafel: i=−i0​exp(RT−αc​Fηs​​):

∂ηs​∂i​=−i0​(RT−αc​F​)exp(RT−αc​Fηs​​)=RTαc​Fi0​​exp(RT−αc​Fηs​​)

@ i=iavg​: iavg​=−i0​exp(RT−αc​Fηs​​)⇒exp(RT−αc​Fηs​​)=−i0​iavg​​

∴∂ηs​∂i​​iavg​​=RTαc​Fi0​​⋅(−i0​iavg​​)=−RTαc​F​iavg​⇒Rct​=(∂ηs​∂i​​i=iavg​​)−1=−αc​FRTiavg​​=Rct​​←Rct​ for cathodic Tafel

Transcriber’s note: as written; inverting the derivative gives RT/(−αc​Fiavg​).

For B-V kinetics:

i=i0​[exp(RTαa​Fηs​​)−exp(RT−αc​Fηs​​)]⇒i0​iavg​​=exp(RTαa​Fη​)−exp(RT−αc​Fη​) ∂ηs​∂i​​=i0​(RTαa​F​)exp(RTαa​Fηs​​)−i0​(RT−αc​F​)exp(RT−αc​Fηs​​)=i0​(RTF​)[αa​exp(RTαa​Fηs​​)+αc​exp(RT−αc​Fηs​​)]=RTFi0​​[αa​(i0​iavg​​+exp(RT−αc​Fηs​​))+αc​exp(RT−αc​Fηs​​)]=RTFi0​​[αa​i0​iavg​​+(αa​+αc​)exp(RT−αc​Fηs​​)]​ Rct​=(∂η∂i​​i=iavg​​)−1=Fi0​[αa​exp(RTαa​Fη​)+αc​exp(RT−αc​Fη​)]RT​​←B-V kinetics Rct​

(b) Graphs shown (plot: B-V kinetics — current density (mA/cm²) vs overpotential from −0.1 to 0.1 V, rising exponentially to ≈350 mA/cm²).

(c) Resistance vs log(i) (three plots of resistance vs log10​(i) for i0​=1, 0.1 and 10, each comparing the linear, anodic Tafel, cathodic Tafel and B-V forms of Rct​).

  • From the above graphs, it seems that the anodic Tafel approximation matches B-V the closest; linear may be accurate only at small current densities but is independent of i.
  • Cathodic diverges at first but then approaches B-V/anodic at higher i0​ values.
  • At higher i0​, the differences reduce (apart from cathodic).
