Problem 2.1

Debye length, with ionic strength Is​:

λ=F2∑j​zj2​cj∞​εRT​​;Is​=21​j∑​zj2​cj∞​∴λ=F2⋅2Is​εRT​​=2F2εRT​​⋅Is−1/2​⇒λ∝Is−1/2​

Net charge falls exponentially over distances on the order of the Debye length → i.e. the distances over which electroneutrality breaks down. (sketch: λ vs Is​, decaying as Is−1/2​.)

@ pH 7, pure water, Is​=1×10−3 M [?]; assuming room temperature of 20 °C:

κ=ε0​ε​;εH2​O​=78⋅(8.854×10−14cmF​)=78×(8.85×10−16mF​)[?] ∴λ=(2×(96485)278×8.854×10−14×8.314×293.15​)1/2⋅(1×10−3×103)−1/2⇒λ=9.5×10−9 m​≈9.5 nm

@ same conditions but Is​=1 M: λ=3.01×10−11 m​≈0.03 nm.

As Is​↑, λ↓ ✓

  • Really small value ✓
  • Significance → the Debye length just provides a general idea of the diffuse layer ↓ a really small value would mean that charge neutrality can only be broken over an nm scale, as the concentration of electrolyte (or Is​) increases. (This might have significance when dealing with double-layer analysis.)

Problem 2.9

(sketch: a 150 mm-long, 1 cm-wide strip; 10 nm resistive Ru substrate with a Cu layer plated over 100 mm of it at 10 mA/cm², current collector at the left end.)

Substrate → Ru (resistive).

(a) Substrate: R=AρL​ (ρ = resistivity) [resistance is proportional to the no. of e⁻ that can pass through a cross-section of wire]

R=(10×10−9)(1×10−2)(71×10−9)(150×10−3)​⇒R=106.5 Ω​

(b) (sketch: 200 nm Cu on top of the 10 nm Ru, 1 cm wide, 150 mm long.)

R=AρL​=(200×10−9)(1×10−2)(17×10−9)(150×10−3)​⇒R=1.3 Ω​ρCu​=17 nΩm

Ideally we would have to consider the resistivity of both Cu & Ru, but because Cu is much more conductive (and thicker), we only need to consider this layer.

(c) Constant deposition rate = 10 mA cm⁻² (current density i); Ohm’s law: V=IR.

Shell balance (sketch: a slab element of width a and thickness a between x and x+Δx, with in​ entering through the top face; m = metal, s = substrate; Vm​=ρim​L):

(im​(x)−im​(x+Δx))a2=−in​Δx2⇒a2dx2dim​​[?]=in​;im​=ρs​Ls​Vm​​ ρs​Ls​a2​dx2d2Vm​​=in​;a2=Ls​⋅λs​⇒ρs​λs​​(dx2d2Vm​​)=in​​

(d) Solve the differential equation:

dx2d2Vm​​=λs​ρs​in​​=f(x);dxdVm​​=λs​ρs​in​​x+c;Vm​=2λs​ρs​in​​x2+cx+d

BCs:

  1. @ x=0, Vm​=0 (potential at the current collector is zero)
  2. @ x=L, dxdVm​​=0 (electronic current at the end is zero)
∴Vm​=λs​2ρs​in​L2​

Results

  • For 10 nm Ru: Vm​=2⋅10×10−9(71×10−9)(100)(150×10−3)2​=8 V
  • For 200 nm Cu: Vm​=2⋅200×10−9(17×10−9)(100)(150×10−3)2​=0.095 V

Although current flow majorly happens via Cu ↓ so the Cu potential is imp/useful.
