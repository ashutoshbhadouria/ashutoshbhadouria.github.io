↳ Exactly 2× the Fickian diffusion. Why? The concentration of anions also has to vary linearly in the BL even though their flux is 0, which imposes an electric field within which the Cu²⁺ ions migrate (i.e. the anions create an E-field such that Cu has 2× the flux).

with t−​+t+​=1 (fractions of the total current). If t+​ is small, migration becomes unimportant!! ★

  • Often we want to add extra ions to the solution (called a supporting electrolyte):
    • ↳ to increase the conductivity (good V);
    • ↳ effect on migration → it decreases the electric field, which means migration is less important (it decreases the flux to the surface!).

Supporting electrolyte (ternary electrolyte)

↳ Add H₂SO₄, which completely dissociates to 2H++SO42−​; Cu plating with CuSO₄ + H₂SO₄ at CCu2+​=0 at the surface. Species: 1 = Cu²⁺, 2 = SO₄²⁻, 3 = H⁺. (Dilute solution: no ion–ion, just ion–solvent interactions.)

Assume: (1) SS; (2) v=0; (3) only Cu²⁺ reacts at the electrode (N2​=N3​=0).

Limiting cases for the governing equations

↳ Solving our transport equations can be cumbersome and require numerical techniques, but we can more easily determine transport behaviour in limiting cases that are often a good first approximation.

1. Uniform electrolyte composition (low current)

↳ When current is applied at the electrode, reactants are consumed, changing the composition (sketch: WE with a concentration boundary layer of thickness δ, and a CE at distance L).

If iavg​/ilim​≪1, consider a uniform electrolyte (ilim​ = the current at which csurf​=0). ★ Even if iavg​/ilim​=0.2, csurf​=80%cbulk​.

  • ↳ If the composition is uniform, how many equations do we solve? Electroneutrality → to solve for the potential ϕ in solution.
  • ↳ As stated before, if ∇cj​=0 the potential is defined by the Laplace equation, ∇2ϕ=0.

In 1D, e.g. dx2d2ϕ​=0, subject to BCs at the electrodes:

BC 1 (WE at x=0):

iavg​=−F∑zj​Nj​=−F∑zj​(zj​uj​Fcj​dxdϕ​+zj​Dj​∇cj​​−cj​v​)⇒iavg​=−κdxdϕ​

Assume cathodic Tafel kinetics (often U gets dropped, assuming that the reference electrode is of the same composition as the WE):

−κdxdϕ​=−i0​exp(RT−αc​F(V−ϕ−U)​)⇒−κdxdϕ​=−i0​exp(RTαc​F(V−ϕ)​)

BC 2 (CE): at x=L, ϕ=0. Integrating (×2) ⇒ ϕ=Ax+B; applying the BCs:

ϕ=κiavg​​(L−x),where iavg​=−i0​exp​RT−αc​F(V−κiavg​L​)​​

We can use ϕ to tell us something about the resistances that are important for electrochemical performance. ↳ Using Ohm’s law, we can define:

  • Charge-transfer resistance (Rct​) → associated with the faradaic process at the electrode (★ the slope of the polarization curve: how much the current changes in response to a small change in overpotential):
Rct​=∂i∂(V−ϕ0​)​​iavg​​;iavg​=−i0​exp(RT−αc​F(V−ϕ)​)⇒(V−ϕ)=αc​FRT​ln(i0​i​)
  • Ohmic resistance (solution resistance) at the electrode ↳ associated with charged species moving in the electrolyte:
RΩ​=∂i∂(ϕ0​)​​iavg​​=∂i∂​(κiavg​​(L−x))=κL​

The ratio Rct​/RΩ​ → the current distribution.

↳ clim​=0 at the electrode surface. ★ Assume tlim​≈0, v=0. At SS, ∇2clim​=0 subject to clim​=0 at x=0 and clim​=cbulk​ at x=δ:
