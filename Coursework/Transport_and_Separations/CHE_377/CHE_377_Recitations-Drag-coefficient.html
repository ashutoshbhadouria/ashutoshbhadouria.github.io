Recitation 09/05 — concept review (typed and handwritten)

Surface tension (typed)

  • Now we are dealing with interactions b/w two fluids at their interface (e.g. bubbles)
  • Phase boundary = “free surface” of the flow
  • Surface tension is a fluid property (like density and viscosity; denoted by σ) and is a result of unbalanced molecular forces at a surface
  • Force is tangent to the surface and normal to the boundary
  • The downward force on surface particles is not balanced by an upward force of fluid on the other side of the interface; the net effect is that the free surface behaves like a thin massless film under tension.

Reynolds transport theorem (typed)

  • Has different forms depending on the frame of reference we are in.

  • We could apply balance laws on each fluid element (box) → but have to deal with awkward shapes.

  • We can define a control volume — an arbitrary shape region that is fixed within the flow and sees fluid coming and going out. (figure: fluid elements deforming as they move through a channel, top view at t=0,t1​,t2​.)

  • Extensive property: depends on the amount of matter in a sample, Ψ. Intensive property: independent of the size of sample, ψ=∂m∂Ψ​ (m = mass).

  • Total material volume corresponding to a mass w/ the property ψ is written as Ψ=∭V(t)​ρψdV (an arbitrary material volume).

Frames of reference: material volume vs. control volume. (sketches: a material blob followed along a path x1​→x2​→x3​, labelled D/Dt; a fixed control volume at position x with streamlines passing through it.)

  • Material volume: flux of a property across a material volume is 0 ↳ i.e. a volume whose boundaries are moving w/ time. Rate of change of a fluid property is given by [DtD​].
  • Control volume: fixed x-position; there is a non-zero flux. Rate of change of a fluid property is given by [∂t∂​].
DtD​[FP]=∂t∂​[FP]+v​⋅∇(FP)​(FP=fluid property;v​=velocity of fluid)

We know Ψ=∭V(t)​ρψdV → this is w/ respect to the material frame of reference ↳ however, the volume is changing, so we cannot evaluate (as we don’t know the limits of integration) ↳ but we know we can go from the material to the control frame of reference via the green equation: DtDΨ​=∂t∂Ψ​+v​⋅∇Ψ=∂t∂​[∭V0​​⋯].

Momentum balance

  • Momentum (p) = mass (m) · velocity (v): p​=mv​; mass = (density)·volume, m=ρV → p​=(ρV)v​ (the m3 term = m2⋅m → area · length).
  • Rate of momentum change: momentum per unit time. “Dividing” by time: p​=(ρ⋅Area⋅l)v​ ≈ Δtp​​=ρ⋅Area⋅Δtl​⋅v​, with Δtl​=v. That is why the momentum balance has two velocity terms!

(figure 5.2: fluid flow through a control volume — streamlines at time t crossing a surface element dA with normal n at angle θ to v.) We care about fluid velocity in the direction of the normal to the surface.

→ Volume of fluid passed through cross-section A (sketch: a slanted cylinder of base A, height unknown, with v at angle θ to n^): Vcylinder​=A⋅Height; height =Δxcosθ (SOH CAH TOA) ∴ Vcylinder​=AΔxcosθ;
