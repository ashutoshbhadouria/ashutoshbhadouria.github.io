Realistic & practical flow

Turbulent flow in smooth pipes

Prandtl correlation (7.156):f​1​=4log[Ref​]−0.4(smooth pipes);Re=μρ⟨v⟩D​;f=2Lρ⟨v⟩2ΔpD​(pipe friction factor from experiments)

Substituting f into Ref​ eliminates ⟨v⟩:

4log[μρ⟨v⟩D​2Lρ⟨v⟩2ΔpD​​]−0.4=f​1​⇒4log[μρD​2LρΔpD​​]−0.4=f​1​⇒"LHS"4log[2Lμ2ρΔpD3​​]−0.4​​=f​1​

Algorithm 1:

  1. Guess Qguess​ (will give ⟨v⟩)
  2. Find Re
  3. Find f1​ (Prandtl correlation)
  4. Find Δp1​ (friction factor)
  5. Calculate Δp2​ = known value − Δp1​
  6. Solve for “LHS” (given Δp2​) → gives f2​ → ⟨v⟩ (using the friction factor)
  7. Solve for Qcalc​ [Qcalc​=⟨v⟩πR2]
  8. Compare this to step 1 Qguess​ → if close then stop ↳ else iterate

Turbulent flow in connected smooth pipes

Example 7.9: a 40-foot section of 1.0-inch ID piping branches into two pipes of the same diameter, one of which is 60.0 feet long and one of which is 85.0 feet long (Figure 7.23). The main pipe is connected to the municipal water supply, which supplies a constant water pressure of 62 psig at the pipe entrance. What are the flow rates through the two pipe exits? What is the pressure at the splitting point? Assume smooth pipes. (figure: p1​=62 psig, Q=Q1​+Q2​ through 40 ft to the split at p2​; 60 ft to p3​=0 psig (Q1​ = ?) and 85 ft to p4​=0 psig (Q2​ = ?).)

Δp2​=P2​−P3​=P2​−P4​.

  1. Guess Δp2​ (“LHS”) → find f (friction factor) → ⟨v⟩ → Q1​, Q2​
  2. Total Q (Q1​+Q2​=Q) → now use Algorithm 1! (until step 5) ↳ Δp1​=p1​−p2​
  3. (Δp2​)calc​=62−Δp1​
  4. Iterate until (Δp2​)calc​≈(Δp2​)guess​

(1) Since we don’t know Q ↳ we first need to solve for Q1​ & Q2​ by guessing a Δp2​ value. (2) Once we know Q → then focus on the small initial 40 ft pipe and calculate the Δp2​ value independently ↓ see if it matches? ↳ if not we iterate!
